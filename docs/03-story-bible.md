# Story Bible — Draft 1

## The world

Near-future, grounded. Nothing in Quantum Heist breaks physics except one
black-box device: the **Echo Rig**. Nobody in the crew can explain how it
works; the one person who could built it and then erased herself from every
record. The Rig shows *honest* futures — and the crew has learned that seeing
a future makes it yours to keep or throw away.

## Factions

### The Kestrel Collective (player crew)
A five-person heist crew that stopped fencing loot and started stealing
*leverage*: ledgers, models, drafts — the documents powerful people pay to
forget. They work clean, leave no bodies, and never take a job that hurts
someone poor. Their handler, **Marrow**, negotiates entirely in voice notes
that arrive seconds before they're needed.

- **Vex** — entry specialist. Talks to locks like they're shy animals.
- **Halcyon** — the planner. Plays the Rig like an instrument; keeps a
  notebook of collapsed timelines she refuses to delete.
- **Rook** — eyes and overwatch. Sees the patrol before the patrol exists.
- **Sable** — ghost. Walks routes the Rig predicted three minutes ago.
- **Ash** — driver and conscience. The one who asks "should we?" before
  "can we?"

### Meridian Solutions (antagonist)
A private-security megacorp whose actual product is *impunity*: they sell
facilities that make evidence impossible. Their flagship sites run **CLEANROOM**,
an audit system that records everything and forgets nothing — the natural
enemy of a deterministic engine, and the in-fiction reason every heist must be
*perfect*: a compromised timeline isn't just a failed job, it's a permanent
record.

### The Archivist (mystery)
Someone is buying Meridian's leaked files before Kestrel can sell them —
and paying in futures. Every time the Rig forks, one branch comes back with
a note inside. The notes are signed with Marrow's voice.

## The Echo Rig (in-fiction rules)

1. **Echoes are honest.** The Rig never shows a lie; it shows *a* future.
2. **Forks cost memory.** Every open branch is a live mind-state the crew
   must carry. Too many forks and the crew starts doubting which timeline is
   theirs (gameplay: fork charges).
3. **Collapse is mercy.** A collapsed timeline didn't happen. The crew treats
   it as a confession they get to take back.
4. **CLEANROOM sees echoes.** Endgame escalation: high-tier facilities
   partially *predict the predictors*. The Rig vs. CLEANROOM arms race is the
   campaign spine.

## Environmental storytelling rules

- Facilities tell you what Meridian protects and why: a records annex full of
  settlement drafts, a lab wing with redacted door plaques.
- Guards' patrol loops mirror their boredom — the veteran routes hug shadows,
  the rookie cuts corners. Players learn *people*, not just patterns.
- Every vault contains one human document (a letter, a photo, a resignation
  draft). The loot is leverage; the story is what it cost someone.

## Campaign shape (season 1)

| Arc | Episodes | Rig feature unlocked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tunnel Run | 1–3 | Predict (echoes) |
| Forked | 4–6 | Fork |
| Collapse | 7–9 | Promote / discard branches |
| CLEANROOM | 10–12 | Divergence markers + adversarial prediction |

Each arc ends with a vault document that recontextualizes the previous one —
the heists assemble the biography of the Rig's missing inventor.
